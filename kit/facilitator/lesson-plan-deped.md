# Detailed lesson plan: can your classmates escape a world you invented?

Draft for Tomer's review. Not tried with children. Timings are targets: nothing here has been timed with a class.

DepEd DLP format (DO 42 s. 2016), still widely used alongside MATATAG. This replaces the earlier MATATAG lesson exemplar version.

In this version the children use Kai's Escape Builder prototype (<https://imagi-mockup.vercel.app/>) to plan and playtest their game, then build it in Lovable inside imagi Edu. Read "Risks with this version" at the end before choosing it.

## I. OBJECTIVES

| | |
|---|---|
| A. Content Standards | The learners demonstrate an understanding of:<br>1. how a small game follows rules: a clue leads to a code; a wrong or empty code keeps the exit shut; a hint points to the clue without giving the code; Restart puts everything back;<br>2. how a clear instruction to an AI builder says what to change, why, and what must stay the same;<br>3. why "Done" from an AI is a claim until a real player tests it. |
| B. Performance Standards | The learners, in pairs, design their own one-screen escape game, have a learner from another pair play it, instruct Lovable in imagi Edu to build it, and improve one thing for a reason they saw in the playtest. |
| C. Learning Competencies | No DepEd code: there is no Grade 7 competency for AI or game design. Written for this lesson and aligned to the CSTA K-12 Computer Science Standards (2017), level 2: 2-AP-13 (decompose a problem into parts), 2-AP-15 (use feedback from users to refine a solution), 2-AP-17 (systematically test and refine), 2-AP-19 (document to make a program easier to test). Also ISTE Students 1.4 and 1.5. The school checks the fit before formal use. |
| Specific objectives | At the end of the lesson, the learners are expected to:<br>a. explain what makes an escape game fair: a clue, a code and a hint that points without telling (knowledge);<br>b. plan their own game by answering ten design questions, and give an AI clear instructions to build it (skill);<br>c. test the game with a player, write one change as Change / Because / Keep / Check, and retest it (skill);<br>d. take turns at the keyboard and watch a classmate play without helping (attitude). |

## IV. PROCEDURES

Keyboard: Partner A is the odd learner number, Partner B the even one. Each pair's Lovable project is named "Escape - pair N", with no real names.

| Procedure | Teacher's activity | Learner's activity |
|---|---|---|
| A. Reviewing previous lesson or presenting the new lesson<br>(0-1, 1 min) | Not applicable: first lesson in the unit.<br>Hook. Say: "Can your classmates escape a world you invented? Today you'll make one." | Listen. Laptops closed. |
| B. Establishing a purpose for the lesson<br>(1-2, 1 min) | Say: "Today you will give an AI clear instructions to build a game you designed. A player will test it, and you will make it better."<br>Write on the board: Plan. Build. Test. Improve. | Read the four words aloud with the teacher. |
| C. Presenting examples/instances of the new lesson<br>(2-4, 2 min) [Projector] | Open Alien Snack Shop. Say: "Someone built this by hand to show the kind of game you'll ask the AI to make. Who wants to try it?" Pick a volunteer by learner number.<br>If the volunteer is stuck: "Want a hint, or one more try?" Never say the code. | The volunteer clicks things in the shop, tries a wrong code, presses Hint, finds the receipt and the lock pictures, types the code and reaches the snack party.<br>The class calls out guesses. |
| D. Discussing new concepts and practicing new skills #1<br>(4-6, 2 min) [Projector] | Ask: "What made that game fair?" Write the learners' words under three headings on the board: a clue you can find; a code that matches the clue; a hint that points without telling.<br>Ask one question and wait: "What if the hint said the code?" | Answer in their own words. Name the clue, the code and the hint in the demo.<br>Say what would happen to the puzzle if the hint gave the code. |
| E. Discussing new concepts and practicing new skills #2<br>(6-14, 8 min; estimated 5 to 10, untimed with children) [Prototype on laptops] | Say: "Open the Escape Builder. Answer the ten questions with your partner. Your idea first. Don't copy the snack shop: this is your world."<br>Partner A types questions 1 to 5, Partner B types 6 to 10.<br>Walk the room. Check each idea is suitable for school. When a pair is stuck, ask one question and wait: "Who will play it? What would make them smile?"<br>For pictures, say: "Pick every thing from the things library. If your thing isn't there, pick the closest picture. Don't type a new thing name." (A typed new name makes the prototype ask an image AI, which children's work must not use.)<br>At minute 12: "Two minutes left." | In pairs, answer the ten questions in their own words:<br>1. the world; 2. who plays; 3. colours and mood; 4. things in the room, each picked from the things library (the closest picture if theirs isn't there; never a typed new name); 5. the locked exit; 6. the secret code; 7. where the clue hides; 8. the hint; 9. the wrong-code message; 10. the ending.<br>Each partner gives at least one answer of their own. |
| F. Developing mastery (leads to formative assessment)<br>(14-16, 2 min) [Prototype] | Say: "Check your plan. Could a stranger solve it? Is the code hidden everywhere except the clue?"<br>Formative check: walk the room with the per-pair sheet and tick two boxes for each pair you reach: (1) a stranger could solve it: the clue is in the room and leads to the code; (2) the code is never shown: not in the hint, not in the wrong-code message.<br>Pairs you don't reach now, check during G. | Read their answers to each other. Check the two things.<br>If one fails, change that answer now. The pair decides how. |
| G. Finding practical applications of concepts and skills in daily living<br>(16-21, 5 min) [Prototype] | Say: "Share your game with a friend. Players, play. Makers, no helping and no pointing. Write what the player did."<br>Pairs 1 and 2 swap, 3 and 4, 5 and 6, 7 and 8. On each laptop, one learner from the other pair plays and one maker watches.<br>Player stuck: "Want a hint, or one more try?" Never give the code. | Makers click "Play it here" in the prototype. The player plays.<br>The watching maker writes on the playtest card: "The player clicked ___ first. They got stuck at ___."<br>The other maker plays the neighbours' game. |
| H. Making generalizations and abstractions about the lesson<br>(21-37, 16 min) [imagi/Lovable] | 21-23. Say: "Copy your brief and send it to Lovable. While it builds, each of you writes one prediction."<br>23-26. Wait for the build (target 3 minutes; not measured for eight pairs at once). No playable game 5 minutes after sending: paper backup.<br>26-28. Say: "Was your prediction right? Play it."<br>28-30. Say: "Same friend, same laptop. Play the Lovable version. Makers, no helping and no pointing. Write what the player did." Player stuck: "Want a hint, or one more try?" Never give the code.<br>30-32. Ask: "What did your player do in the Lovable game? Which change helps them? Why that one?"<br>32-36. Say: "Test the change, then one thing that should still work." Then: "Lovable says it's done. What happened when you tried it?"<br>36-37. Sum up with the class. Ask: "What did Lovable do that you didn't mean?" "When did you know it worked?" Write: "AI does what you say, not what you mean." "Done is a claim until you test it." | 21-23. Partner A: in the prototype, click "Copy my Lovable prompt". In imagi Edu, open Lovable, start a project named "Escape - pair N", paste and send.<br>23-26. Both write: "When I type a wrong code, I think the game will ___."<br>26-28. Partner A plays Version 1: things, a wrong code, an empty code, Hint, the right code, Restart.<br>28-30. Same swap as in G: the same friend plays the Lovable version for about 2 minutes. The maker who watched in G watches again and writes on the playtest card what the player did in the Lovable game.<br>30-32. The pair picks one change from what their player did in the Lovable game and writes Change / Because / Keep / Check on the playtest card.<br>32-36. Partner B types it into Lovable and sends. Test the change, then a kept rule. Write the result.<br>36-37. Answer in their own words. |
| I. Evaluating learning<br>(37-43, 6 min) [Paper] | Hand out the quiz. Say: "On your own. Write what you did." Read any item aloud if asked, and note "read aloud" on the sheet.<br>Score with the answer key below. | Answer the five items alone. No partner, no AI. |
| J. Additional activities for application or remediation<br>(43-45, 2 min) | Say: "Next time you build a different kind of game." Show the challenge ladder: a second clue; a click-in-order rule; two players taking turns on one laptop (no second device, no link); freestyle.<br>Remediation: learners below 8 of 10 retest their own game with a new player next lesson, using the playtest card. | Close the Lovable tab.<br>Write one next idea on the playtest card. |

Total: 1 + 1 + 2 + 2 + 8 + 2 + 5 + 16 + 6 + 2 = 45 minutes.

### Quiz (I. Evaluating learning)

Learner number: ______

1. Name one thing Lovable did that was different from what you asked. If it did everything you asked, name one thing you checked.

______

2. How did you test your game? Write one thing you did and what you expected to happen.

______

3. What did your player do? What did you change because of it?

______

4. Which instruction is clearer for the AI? Circle one. Then say why.

(a) "Make the game better."

(b) "Change: make the hint point to the shelf. Keep: the code and the ending."

______

5. Why must the hint not give the code?

______

### Answer key: 0 to 2 points per item, 10 in all

| Item | 2 points | 1 point | 0 points |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Names a real difference and where it showed, or names a thing they checked and what they saw | Names a difference or a check, with no detail | Blank, or "nothing" with no check named |
| 2 | An action and the result they expected, such as "I typed a wrong code and expected the door to stay shut" | An action only, such as "I typed a code" | "I tested it" or blank |
| 3 | What the player did and a change that answers it | A change with no link to the player, or the player with no change | Blank or unrelated |
| 4 | Circles (b) and says it names what to change and what to keep | Circles (b) with no reason, or a weak reason | Circles (a) |
| 5 | The puzzle disappears or the player stops thinking, and a hint should point to where to look | One of those ideas | Blank or unrelated |

Item 1 must not push a learner to invent a problem. If Lovable did what they asked, a named check earns full marks.

80% mark for VI.A: 8 of 10 points. A classroom quiz, not piloted and not a validated measure. This version does not run checks A and B; see risk 6.

## Risks with this version

For Kai and Tomer, before choosing it.

| # | Risk | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The class depends on a second site outside imagi: the prototype. If the school network blocks it, or it is down, steps E to G stop | Check the site on a school laptop during prep. Fallback: the paper idea card ([kit/student/idea-card.md](../student/idea-card.md)) and Tomer's flow |
| 2 | The ten questions were estimated at 5 to 10 minutes. Nobody has timed them with children | Time them with one or two children before a class |
| 3 | The prototype asks an image AI to draw names its library lacks. AGENTS.md says children's games never include live image generation | In class, children pick every thing from the things library. If a thing isn't there, they pick the closest library picture. They never type a new thing name. This is an instruction only: the prototype doesn't enforce it yet (a library-only switch would be next-block work). Whether the prototype is used in class at all is Tomer's decision |
| 4 | Saving is browser storage on one device. A shared laptop, a cleared browser or another pair can lose or see the plan | Send to Lovable in the same lesson. Don't promise children the plan will be there next time |
| 5 | 45 minutes is tight for ten questions, a playtest, a Lovable build, a change and a quiz | Use the cut list below |
| 6 | reference/assignment.md asks for two independent checks (A and B) and a first playable game by minute 10. This version replaces the checks with the quiz, and the first Lovable build is sent at minute 21, so the first Lovable game arrives around minute 26 | Tomer decides whether this is acceptable for an option. If not, use the CSTA plan or Tomer's flow |

Cut list, in order. Drop the first one first:

1. J: say the next-lesson line only (saves 1 minute).
2. H sum-up: the teacher says the two lines; no discussion (saves about half a minute).
3. D: ask "What made it fair?" during C instead (saves 2 minutes).
4. I: quiz items 2, 4 and 5 only, out of 6; the 80% mark becomes 5 of 6 (saves 3 minutes).
5. H Version 2: pairs write Change / Because / Keep / Check on the card and send it next lesson (saves about 6 minutes).

Never cut: the children's own answers, the playtest in G, and the test of whatever Lovable built.

Not tested yet: whether the prototype hands over a brief before all ten questions are answered.
